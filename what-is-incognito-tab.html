What is Incognito Tab?



During our daily web surfing many of us choose incognito tab/mode in web browsers for private browsing, avoiding tracking and privacy.

But have you ever thought that what this ‘Incognito Tab’ really is.

Is it that private what we often think, does it make us anonymous on the internet while using it?

People use it everyday but still many of us are unaware of how this incognito tab works? What is the logic behind making it? And the process involved in making you ‘private’ on internet.

To understand this concept in detail we will have to go through a set of terminologies related to privacy on internet.

Understanding Privacy on Internet

Before understanding an incognito tab it is very important to know that what does it mean if term ‘Privacy’ is used in regard to internet.

First of all there is nothing like absolute privacy or complete anonymity on internet rather there are factors to prevent exposure.

Privacy on internet is not about being anonymous from the world but it is to control who can access, collect or link information about you online or who can trace your online presence.

Using encryption, internet safety protocols and many more, reduce the risk of privacy breach or unwanted exposure on internet.

One thing that everyone should know that while being active on internet two things play a major role shaping the trace of our information - convenience and privacy.

Most of the people who use internet services need quick response and fast loading and convenient internet experience unaware of consequences regarding their online data.

More convenience means more shared data - less privacy, while more privacy means less shared data but the trade off is convenience which is not possible for all of us.

To know more about its working you will have to understand the concept of how web pages are made.

Incognito Tab

Between this convenience and privacy incognito mode/tab balances both by providing local privacy to user.

Incognito tab enables user to stay hidden on its local device or the device he is using, it doesn’t save history, restricts cookies prevents certain trackers making the internet experience convenient for everyday use.

It allows logging, downloads and streaming while being hidden locally preventing browsing traces, form data and cookies.

But it too has limitations; it doesn’t provide network level privacy and your activities are still visible to you ISP and IP address can be traced using certain methods.

How Does Incognito Tab Works

To understand how an incognito tab works, it is important to know what changes inside the browser when a private browsing session is started. Unlike normal browsing, it follows a different approach to storing user data.

Instead of making you invisible on the internet, Incognito Mode creates a temporary browsing session that limits the storage of certain information on your local device.

Let us understand the process involved behind it.

1. Normal Browsing

To understand how Incognito Mode works, we must first understand how a normal browsing session works.

Every time you open a website in a regular browser window, the browser stores different types of information on your device to improve speed, convenience, and the overall browsing experience.

These records help websites remember your preferences, keep you signed in, and load pages faster when you visit them again.

Key Notes:

  • Browsing history is stored - Every website you visit is recorded in your browser's history until it is manually cleared.

  • Cookies are saved - Websites store cookies to remember login sessions, preferences, and other user-specific information.

  • Cached files are retained - Images, scripts, and other website resources are stored locally to improve loading speed during future visits.

  • Credentials and form data may be stored - If enabled, the browser can save usernames, passwords, and autofill information, which remain accessible through the browser unless removed.

  • Local browsing data can be recovered - If the device or browser profile is compromised, stored browsing data such as history, cookies, cached files, and saved credentials may be accessed by an unauthorized user.

2. Incognito Browsing

Unlike a normal browsing session, Incognito Mode launches an isolated and temporary browser session that operates independently of the primary browser profile.

Instead of writing most browsing artifacts to the persistent user profile, the browser stores session-specific data in temporary memory or isolated storage, which is discarded when all Incognito windows are closed.

Although the browser isolates local session data, the underlying networking process remains unchanged.

Incognito Mode only changes how the browser manages local data storage; it does not alter how your device communicates with websites or the internet.

Key Notes:

  • A temporary browser profile is created - Incognito Mode launches an isolated browsing session separate from the primary browser profile.

  • Session data is stored temporarily - Cookies, cache, and other session-specific data remain available only until all Incognito windows are closed.

  • No browsing records - Browsing history, cookies, and form data are not written to the permanent browser profile after the session ends.

  • Network protocols remain unchanged - DNS resolution, TCP/IP communication, HTTPS encryption, and the HTTP request-response cycle operate exactly as they do during normal browsing.

  • Identity on the network is not hidden - Your IP address, ISP, network administrator, and visited websites can still identify and monitor network traffic through standard internet protocols.



Analogy:

The Incognito Mode is like working on a whiteboard where You can write, erase, and make changes during the session and once the whiteboard is wiped clean, all those notes disappear. But, anyone who is watching you write can still see what you are doing.

Understanding Browser Components

Before moving on to understanding the levels of privacy you can get on internet as well as what level of privacy incognito tabs provide its important to know what does your browsers stores about you.

These are components of browser which store information about user’s profile to add convenience and prepare recommendations for the user.

Browsing History

Browsing history is a chronological record of the websites and web pages you visit while using a browser.

Every time a page is opened, the browser stores information such as the page title, URL, and the date and time of the visit to make it easier to revisit previously accessed websites.

This information is stored locally on your device and it will be there if not cleared manually or you can opt not to save it using settings and configurations

Cookies

Cookies are small text files created by websites and stored by the browser on your local device.

Depending on their purpose, cookies may be temporary (session cookies) or remain stored until they expire or are manually deleted (persistent cookies).

They contain information such as session identifiers, user preferences, language settings, and other data that help websites recognize your browser during future visits.

Form Data

Form data means all the information that a user enters during a session into web forms, such as names, email addresses, phone numbers, addresses, and search queries.

Modern browsers can store this information locally to provide autofill suggestions, reducing the need to enter the same details repeatedly you can disable autofill to not save form data.

Cache

Cache is a small in size but very fast in speed; temporary storage memory used by a web browser to save frequently accessed website resources such as images, CSS files, JavaScript files, fonts, and other static content.

Instead of downloading these resources every time a website is opened, the browser retrieves them from the local cache whenever possible.

The browser automatically updates or replaces cached files when newer versions become available or when the existing cache is cleared.

Network Terminologies Explained

Now, as you know about the components of browser its time to understand network terminologies as the browser doesn’t work alone every request made on internet travels through different network components and channels and protocols.

It will help you understand what incognito mode can and cannot hide.

Lets Understand them one-by-one:

1. IP Address

People know this term and often hear about it but don’t know what it means in technical aspects.

An Internet Protocol (IP) address is a unique numerical identifier assigned to a device connected to a network.

It acts as the device's network address, allowing data packets to be routed between your device and web servers over the internet.

Whenever the user visits a website, IP address is included in the communication so that the server knows where to send the requested data.

It is important to understand that: Incognito Mode prevents certain data from being stored locally, it does not hide or change your IP address, which remains visible to websites.

2. Internet Service Providers

An Internet Service Provider (ISP) is a company that provides users with access to the internet through technologies such as fiber, broadband, mobile data, or satellite connections.

Every request that you make on internet goes through your ISP server before giving you the output.

Incognito Mode does not prevent your ISP from knowing that your device has connected to a particular website.

Since the ISP handles your network traffic, it can see information such as the websites you connect to, your IP address, connection time, and the amount of data transferred.

3. Domain Name System

The Domain Name System (DNS) is a distributed naming system that translates human-readable domain names, such as developerrps.github.io, google.com.

Incognito mode cannot bypass or encrypt DNS by itself.

It means that DNS requests are still visible to your Internet Service Provider (ISP), network administrator, or the configured DNS resolver.

To bypass DNS additional privacy technologies can be used.

4. HTTP and HTTPS Protocols

HTTP (HyperText Transfer Protocol) and HTTPS (HyperText Transfer Protocol Secure) both are the communication protocols used by web browsers.

Both protocols defines how requests are sent by a browser and how responses are returned by a web server.

HTTPS (HyperText Transfer Protocol Secure) is the encrypted version of HTTP that uses Transport Layer Security (TLS) to protect data while it is transmitted over the network.

But even in Incognito Mode: HTTPS does not hide your IP address, DNS requests, or the fact that your device has connected to a particular website.

5. Browser Fingerprinting

Browser fingerprinting is a tracking technique that identifies a browser by collecting information about its configuration instead of relying on cookies.

Websites combine attributes such as the browser version, operating system, screen resolution, installed fonts, language settings, time zone, graphics capabilities, and other device characteristics to create a unique browser fingerprint.

As, Incognito Mode does not significantly change your browser's configuration, it offers limited protection against fingerprinting, and websites may still be able to recognize or distinguish your browser using these characteristics.

Privacy Levels on Internet

Privacy on the internet is not controlled by a single technology or browser feature.

It exists at different levels, where each level protects a different part of your online activity.

Understanding these privacy levels makes it easier to know what Incognito Mode actually protects and where its limitations begin.

1. Device-Level Privacy

Device-level privacy refers to protecting browsing information stored on the local device(your personal phone which you are currently using).

It prevents other users of the same computer or phone from easily viewing browsing history, cookies, cached files, and other locally stored data.

Incognito Mode primarily provides device-level privacy by preventing most browsing data from being permanently stored after the session ends.

2. Browser-Level Privacy

Browser-level privacy is how a web browser collects, stores, and manages browsing data during an internet session.

It focuses on information such as browsing history, cookies, cache, form data, and other records created by the browser itself.

Incognito Mode mainly operates at this level by isolating the browsing session and preventing most of this data from being permanently stored in the browser profile.

3. Network-Level Privacy

Network-level privacy refers to protecting information while it travels between your device and the destination server over the internet.

This includes network identifiers such as your IP address, DNS requests, connection metadata, and traffic passing through your Internet Service Provider (ISP) or network administrator.

Incognito Mode does not provide network-level privacy because it does not encrypt or reroute your internet connection.

4. Website-Level Privacy

Website-level privacy means the information that a website can collect when the user interacts with its services.

This includes your IP address, browser fingerprint, account activity, server logs, analytics data, and other information generated during your visit.

Incognito Mode offers limited website-level privacy.

5. Account-Level Privacy

Account-level privacy refers to the information associated with your online accounts when you sign in to a website or service. If you do get authenticated even once, the service can identify you through your account regardless of the browser mode being used.

Common Myths About Incognito Tab

There are many misconceptions among people regarding the incognito tab, as now you know about all of its aspects lets bust some myths that are still prevailing.

  • Myth:1- Incognito Tab Can Make You Anonymous

    Reality: Incognito tab doesn’t makes you anonymous, your IP Address and activity can still be seen by websites.

  • Myth:2- Incognito Modes Blocks Tracking

    Reality: Incognito Mode deletes the cookies after session ends but it cannot block trackers specially server-side tracking.

  • Myth:3- Incognito Mode Deletes Everything

    Reality: Every Bookmark and Download that is made while using the incognito mode remains saved on your device.

  • Myth:4- Complete Anonymity is Possible on Internet

    Reality: There is nothing like complete anonymity on internet any session and its data can be tracked.
